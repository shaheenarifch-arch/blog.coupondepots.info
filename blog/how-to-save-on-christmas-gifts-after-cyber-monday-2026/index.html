Quick answer

You can still save a lot on Christmas gifts after Cyber Monday. Many stores keep running sales through December, with another push around Green Monday (December 14, 2026) and in the days before carrier shipping cut-offs. To save: check a coupon site such as CouponDepots before every checkout, set price-drop alerts on the gifts you want, hit free-shipping thresholds instead of paying for delivery, stack store sales with cashback and discounted gift cards, and order well before the official holiday shipping deadlines published by USPS, UPS and FedEx. After the deadlines, switch to buy online, pick up in store, or give digital gift cards and experiences.

If you missed Black Friday and Cyber Monday, or you still have half your list to buy, do not panic and do not pay full price. The idea that every good deal disappears at midnight on Cyber Monday is mostly marketing. In reality, December is full of sales: retailers want to clear holiday stock, hit year-end targets and win shoppers who are still deciding.

The challenge in December is different from November. You are racing two clocks: the price clock (when is this item at its lowest?) and the shipping clock (will it arrive by Christmas?). This guide gives you a step-by-step plan for both, along with the tools, timing and checkout habits that consistently save money.

Key takeaways

  • December sales continue after Cyber Monday, with notable pushes around Green Monday and just before shipping cut-offs.
  • Check for a working coupon code before every checkout, and test codes quickly instead of hunting for "secret" ones.
  • Use price-drop alerts and price history to avoid fake "sale" prices.
  • Stack savings: sale price + one code + cashback + discounted gift cards + loyalty points.
  • Know the shipping deadlines, then switch to in-store pickup and digital gifts when they pass.

Are there still good deals after Cyber Monday?

Yes. The biggest doorbuster discounts are concentrated in Black Friday week, but many retailers:

  • Extend Cyber Monday sales for a few days ("Cyber Week").
  • Run themed December events, such as 12-days-of-deals promotions.
  • Discount again around Green Monday, the second Monday of December, which falls on December 14, 2026.
  • Offer free shipping promotions in mid-December to win last-minute orders.
  • Mark down holiday-specific stock (decorations, gift sets, seasonal clothing) as Christmas approaches.

Some categories also follow their own cycles. Toys, for example, can see deals close to Christmas as retailers clear shelves, while popular electronics may sell out rather than drop further. If an item you want is at a genuinely good price now, waiting for a slightly better one is a gamble.

Your December savings plan, week by week

Week of December 1: Cyber Week leftovers

  • Revisit your list and check which Cyber Monday deals are still running.
  • Check stores' "Cyber Week" or "extended deals" pages.
  • Set up price alerts on everything you have not bought yet.

Week of December 7: compare and commit

  • Compare prices across two or three stores for each big item.
  • Check coupon sites for current codes on stores you plan to use.
  • Buy anything shipping from far away or from smaller sellers now.

Week of December 14: Green Monday and free shipping pushes

  • Watch for Green Monday promotions on December 14.
  • Look for free-shipping promotions and order before standard ground deadlines.
  • Buy gifts that need personalization or engraving now at the latest.

Week of December 21: last-minute mode

  • Use buy online, pick up in store (BOPIS) or curbside pickup.
  • Choose same-day delivery where available and worth the fee.
  • Give digital gift cards, subscriptions, tickets or experiences.

How to find coupon codes that actually work

Most frustration with coupons comes from expired, fake or restricted codes. A few habits save time.

  1. Start on a coupon site you trust. Check CouponDepots for the featured brands and current codes before you check out, and look at when a code was last verified.
  2. Read the terms before you test. Many codes exclude sale items, gift cards or certain brands, or require a minimum spend.
  3. Test quickly. Try the top two or three codes; if none work, move on rather than trying twenty.
  4. Check the store's own sources. Email sign-up discounts, app-only codes and loyalty offers are often better than public codes.
  5. Avoid suspicious "100% off" codes and pop-ups that ask for personal information. The FTC's online shopping guidance is a good refresher on safe checkout habits.

Use price tracking to avoid fake sales

A "50% off" banner means little if the "original" price was inflated a week earlier. Before buying a big-ticket gift:

  • Check price history using a browser extension or price-tracking site.
  • Set a target price and an alert; buy when it hits.
  • Compare like for like: model numbers, sizes, bundles and warranties.
  • Ask about price adjustments. Some stores refund the difference if the price drops within a short window after purchase. Check the policy before you rely on it.

Stack savings the smart way

Most stores allow only one promo code per order, but you can still layer several savings on the same purchase:

  1. Start with the sale price.
  2. Apply the best single promo code the store accepts.
  3. Reach the free-shipping threshold with something you actually need rather than paying for shipping.
  4. Shop through a cashback portal or card-linked offer if you use one.
  5. Pay with a discounted gift card for that store, where available from reputable sources.
  6. Use loyalty points or rewards you have already earned.

Holiday shipping deadlines: how to plan

Carriers publish recommended holiday shipping deadlines every year, usually from mid-December, with the latest dates for ground, priority and express services. Retailers then set their own order cut-offs, which are often earlier than the carrier dates because orders take time to process.

  • Check the official holiday pages for USPS, UPS and FedEx for this year's dates.
  • Check each retailer's holiday cut-off on its shipping or FAQ page.
  • Give yourself a buffer of two to three days, especially for rural addresses and oversized items.
  • Ship gifts to family directly from the retailer to save a trip to the post office, and add a gift receipt.

Protect yourself from holiday shopping scams

December is peak season for scams. Keep your savings safe:

  • Shop on secure sites you know, and type addresses yourself rather than clicking unexpected links.
  • Be wary of deals that look too good to be true, especially on social media ads from unfamiliar stores.
  • Use a credit card for online purchases where possible; it usually offers stronger fraud protection.
  • Ignore delivery-problem texts and emails that ask you to click a link or pay a fee. Check tracking directly on the carrier's site.
  • Never pay with gift cards when someone asks you to settle a "bill" or "fee." The FTC warns this is a hallmark of scams.

Budget tips for the rest of the season

  1. Set a total budget and a per-person amount, and track spending in a simple list or spreadsheet.
  2. Prioritize the gifts that matter most and look for deals on those first.
  3. Avoid impulse "add-ons" to reach thresholds unless you would buy them anyway.
  4. Keep receipts and gift receipts in one email folder for returns.
  5. Check return windows. Many stores extend holiday returns into January; confirm before you buy.

December deals by category

Different categories behave differently in December. Use these patterns as a guide, then confirm with price history.

Electronics

Popular headphones, tablets and smart home devices often see repeat promotions in December, but the most in-demand models can sell out. If the price is close to its recent low, buy rather than wait. Double-check model numbers; some "deal" versions are older or made specifically for sales events.

Toys and games

Toy prices can move a lot in December as retailers react to demand. Popular toys may sell out early, while less-hyped items get marked down closer to Christmas. Buy the "must-have" gift early and leave stocking stuffers for later.

Clothing and shoes

Fashion retailers run frequent December promotions, often with extra percentage-off codes. Check size availability early, and look for free returns in case a gift does not fit.

Beauty and gift sets

Holiday gift sets are designed as presents and are often good value compared with buying items separately. Many are discounted as Christmas approaches.

Home and kitchen

Small appliances, cookware and bedding regularly appear in December sales. These are also good candidates for free-shipping thresholds.

Experiences and subscriptions

Gift cards for experiences, streaming or learning subscriptions and memberships often come with bonus offers in December and arrive instantly.

Shopping for specific people on a budget

  • Parents and grandparents: Photo gifts (order early), cozy home items, experiences you can share.
  • Partners: Something they mentioned in passing, an experience, or an upgrade of something they use daily.
  • Teens: Gift cards, earbuds, clothing from their favorite store (with a gift receipt).
  • Young children: One main toy plus books and art supplies, which are often discounted.
  • Coworkers and teachers: Small, thoughtful items under a set budget, such as quality chocolates, a candle or a gift card for coffee.
  • Hosts: A nice bottle of olive oil, a plant, or a small food gift.

Returns, exchanges and price adjustments

Before you buy, check:

  1. Holiday return windows. Many retailers extend return deadlines for items bought in November and December into January. Confirm the exact date for each store.
  2. Gift receipts. Ask for one at checkout or add it to online orders so recipients can exchange easily.
  3. Price adjustment policies. Some stores refund the difference if the price drops within a set period. Others do not offer adjustments during the holidays.
  4. Return shipping costs. Free returns can make online shopping much less risky.
  5. Final sale exclusions. Clearance and some sale items cannot be returned.

Wrapping, shipping and gifting cheaper

  • Ship directly to the recipient when retailers offer free gift messages and gift receipts.
  • Combine orders to reach free-shipping thresholds.
  • Use flat-rate boxes for heavy gifts you are sending yourself, and compare carrier prices.
  • Reuse gift bags and boxes, and buy wrapping paper after Christmas for next year.
  • Send e-gift cards to faraway friends instead of shipping physical presents.

A simple holiday budget tracker

Keep a list with five columns: name, budget, gift idea, price paid and status (ordered, shipped, wrapped). Updating it after each purchase stops overspending and makes sure nobody is forgotten. Total the "price paid" column every few days and compare it with your overall budget.

Using apps and alerts to catch December price drops

You do not need to check stores every day. Let technology do the watching:

  • Store apps: Many retailers post app-only deals and send alerts for items on your wish list.
  • Wish lists and carts: Add items to your cart or wish list; some stores send a discount or reminder when prices drop.
  • Price-tracking extensions: Set a target price and get an email when a product reaches it.
  • Email sign-ups: New subscribers often receive a welcome code. Use a separate email folder or address to keep promotional emails organised.
  • Coupon sites: Bookmark CouponDepots and check it before you pay for anything.

Turn off alerts after the holidays so you are not tempted into unplanned spending in January.

Last-minute gift ideas that still feel thoughtful

If it is December 23 and you still need gifts, these ideas feel personal rather than rushed:

  1. A planned experience: A dinner, a class, a concert or a day trip you will do together, presented with a handwritten card.
  2. A subscription: Streaming, audiobooks, a magazine or a learning platform matched to their interests.
  3. A donation in their name to a cause they care about.
  4. A digital gift card to their favorite store or restaurant, paired with a note about why you chose it.
  5. A homemade gift: Baked goods, a playlist, a photo book (if a same-day print service is available near you) or a voucher for your time and help.

How to avoid overspending in December

December sales are designed to make you buy more than you planned. A few guardrails help:

  • Shop from your list, not from deal emails.
  • Set a cap for "treat yourself" purchases if you know you will be tempted.
  • Wait 24 hours before buying anything over a set amount that was not on your list.
  • Avoid "buy now, pay later" for gifts unless you are certain you can pay on time; missed payments can add fees.
  • Remember the January sales. Many items you want for yourself will be discounted again after Christmas.

The takeaway: in December, speed and planning matter more than finding the single biggest percentage off. A good price on the right gift that arrives on time beats a slightly better price on something that arrives after Christmas.

A December checklist you can copy

  • Make a gift list with a budget for each person.
  • Set price alerts on every item not yet bought.
  • Check CouponDepots and store apps before each checkout.
  • Note Green Monday (December 14) and carrier deadline dates in your calendar.
  • Order anything personalized or from small sellers first.
  • Switch to in-store pickup and digital gifts after the deadlines.
  • Keep every receipt and gift receipt in one email folder.

One more tip: check the total, not the percentage

A bigger percentage off does not always mean a cheaper gift. Before you pay, compare the final checkout total across stores, including shipping, taxes, any service fees and the value of free returns. A 30% discount with free shipping and free returns can beat a 40% discount with paid shipping and a strict return policy. Taking 30 seconds to compare totals is one of the simplest ways to make sure a December deal really is a deal.

Frequently asked questions

Are prices lower on Cyber Monday or in December?

Many of the deepest doorbuster deals happen in Black Friday week, but plenty of December sales match or beat them on specific items. Use price history and alerts rather than assuming one day is always cheapest.

When is Green Monday in 2026?

Green Monday falls on the second Monday of December, which is December 14, 2026. Many online retailers run promotions around that date.

How can I find coupon codes that actually work?

Start with a trusted coupon site such as CouponDepots, read each code's terms, test the top two or three quickly, and check store emails and apps for exclusive codes.

What are the holiday shipping deadlines for 2026?

Carriers publish their recommended dates in their holiday guides, and retailers set their own, often earlier, order cut-offs. Check USPS, UPS, FedEx and each retailer's shipping page, and order a few days early.

What should I do if I miss the shipping deadline?

Use buy online, pick up in store, same-day delivery where available, or give digital gift cards, subscriptions or experiences that arrive instantly.

Can I use more than one coupon code on an order?

Most stores accept only one promo code per order, but you can still stack savings with sale prices, free shipping, cashback, discounted gift cards and loyalty points.

Is it safe to buy discounted gift cards?

Only from the retailer itself or reputable, well-known sellers. Avoid unofficial marketplaces and never pay any bill or "fee" with gift cards.

The bottom line

Missing Cyber Monday does not mean paying full price. In December, combine a working coupon code with price-drop alerts, free shipping and cashback, watch for Green Monday on December 14, and order before the carrier deadlines. When the clock runs out, switch to in-store pickup and digital gifts. Check CouponDepots before every checkout and you will keep more of your holiday budget.
